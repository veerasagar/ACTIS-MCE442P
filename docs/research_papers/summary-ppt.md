# Literature Survey: Findings and Limitations

This alternative literature survey table formats all 38 core project references according to the requested evaluation metrics, focusing specifically on their key attributes, findings, and documented or inherent challenges relevant to the proposed ACTIS architecture.

| SL No | Source (Latest) | Paper Details | Key Attributes/Findings | Challenges / Limitations |
| :---: | :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **1** | **arXiv**<br>Jan 2026 | **Tri-LLM Cooperative Federated Zero-Shot Intrusion Detection...**<br>*Zhang, Yang, et al.* | Employs multi-LLM semantic disagreement to detect zero-day attacks without data sharing. Introduces "Trust-Aware" local client weighting. | LLMs act as static encoders, not autonomous agents. Lacks privacy-preserving measures for the semantic embeddings and offers no active RAG explainability. |
| **2** | **arXiv**<br>Dec 2025 | **ReGAIN: Retrieval-Grounded AI Framework for Network Traffic...**<br>*Anonymous* | First multi-collection vector database pipeline mapping raw NetFlow directly to actionable intelligence. LLM cites references structurally. | Entirely centralized processing. No federated resilience against data poisoning, and highly vulnerable if the core database is compromised. |
| **3** | **arXiv**<br>Sep 2025 | **CyberRAG: An Agentic RAG Cyber Attack Classification...**<br>*Yuan, Zheng, et al.* | Core LLM agent dynamically calls specialized BERT classifiers per-attack format. Uses MMR retrieval to synthesize final analyst reports. | Limited strictly to Web payloads (SQLi, XSS, SSTI). Agent pipeline lacks any PII stripping mechanisms before querying external intelligence graphs. |
| 4 | **IEEE Access**<br>2024 | **Federated Learning based NIDS using Unbalanced IoT Data**<br>*Tariq et al.* | Proves FL successfully manages extreme class imbalances (e.g., rare BotNet traffic vs normal) by synthesizing minor classes locally. | Focuses primarily on label distribution skew rather than feature shift; vulnerable to malicious Byzantine nodes deliberately mislabeling data. |
| 5 | **IEEE Access**<br>2024 | **Large Language Models in Cybersecurity: Systematic Review**<br>*Wu, Lee, et al.* | Comprehensive survey confirming LLMs excel at log summarization but require strict contextual grounding to prevent hallucinations. | Foundation models struggle with strict syntactic structures (like valid perimeter defense rule syntax) without specific agentic guardrails. |
| 6 | **IEEE TIFS**<br>2024 | **Agent-Based Autonomous Threat Landscape Analysis using LLMs**<br>*Kim & Cho* | Introduces an orchestrator-agent framework that actively maps ongoing attacks to MITRE ATT&CK vectors autonomously. | The agentic workflows face severe latency constraints, making them unsuitable for real-time packet-level, inline network blocking. |
| 7 | **ACM TPS**<br>2024 | **Evaluating the Privacy of Large Language Model Ecosystems**<br>*Huang et al.* | Measures the exact rates at which base LLMs accidentally regurgitate Personally Identifiable Information from training sets or context windows. | Highlights that prompting "do not reveal IP" fails often; strict secondary deterministic validators (Regex loops) are mandatory. |
| 8 | **IEEE TDSC**<br>2024 | **Empowering Zero-Day Attack Discovery using Context-Aware GPTs**<br>*Wang et al.* | Shows that pre-trained attention heads can link slightly mutated network payloads to root attack families zero-shot. | Pure generative discovery lacks explainability compared to retrieving specific historical threat profiles. |
| 9 | **IEEE TKDE**<br>2024 | **PII Sanitization in Large Language Models: Methodologies...**<br>*Lin et al.* | Categorizes state-of-the-art PII redaction methods, demonstrating that multi-agent "red-teaming" (one hides, one seeks) yields best redaction. | High computational overhead to run contradictory loops for every single payload metric generated. |
| 10 | **IEEE TIFS**<br>2024 | **RAG-Sec: Enhancing Zero-Day Vulnerability Analysis using RAG**<br>*Hu et al.* | RAG significantly lowers false-positive rates when diagnosing novel vulnerabilities compared to raw supervised learning classification. | Relies heavily on the freshness of the external database; outdated embeddings result in completely degraded generative output. |
| 11 | **IEEE IoT**<br>2024 | **Synergizing FL and Vector Databases for Decentralized Cyber Immunity**<br>*Zhang, Yang, et al.* | Conceptually proposes linking distinct FL nodes via shared vector-semantic updates of identified threats rather than raw data. | Abstract architectural mapping; does not provide formal privacy bounds on the shared vector states mathematically. |
| 12 | **IEEE TIFS**<br>2023 | **A Federated Learning Approach to Network Intrusion Detection**<br>*Chen et al.* | Establishes the baseline architecture for multi-node MLP intrusion detection on standardized networking datasets via FedAvg. | Local data poisoning by one node quickly propagates bias into the global model under standard averaging. |
| 13 | **IEEE TDSC**<br>2023 | **Collaborative & Privacy-Preserving Intrusion Detection in Edge Computing**<br>*Reynaud, Gupta, et al.* | Adjusts FL to operate effectively on constrained Edge devices with limited RAM/GPU profiles. | Highly sensitive to straggler nodes; connection drops from IoT endpoints delay global federated aggregation rounds. |
| 14 | **IEEE IoT**<br>2023 | **Scalable Privacy-Preserving FL Framework for Securing IoT Devices**<br>*Liu et al.* | Implements distributed hash tables alongside FL to scale the number of participating endpoint agents securely. | Scaling mechanisms increase overall network overhead and bandwidth saturation during global updates. |
| 15 | **IEEE TNNLS**<br>2023 | **Federated Learning with Non-IID Data: A Survey**<br>*Zhao et al.* | Formalizes the statistical feature drift problem where varying network traffic distributions cause global model divergence. | Foundational analysis showing exactly why pure FedAvg fails in network security contexts. |
| 16 | **IEEE TIFS**<br>2023 | **Dynamic Trust-Aware Federated Learning in Unreliable Environments**<br>*Huang, Zhu, et al.* | Generates reliability "trust" scores for nodes based on their training stability, isolating erratic gradients from the average. | Trust scores can be manipulated if an attacker gradually degrades their submissions rather than doing it impulsively. |
| 17 | **IEEE TKDE**<br>2023 | **Differentially Private Vector Embeddings for Secure Retrieval**<br>*Wang, Huang, Dai* | Proves mathematical DP limits via noise injection directly to semantic text output. | Noise injection causes a measurable drop in semantic retrieval accuracy (MMR match degradation). |
| 18 | **ACM TPS**<br>2023 | **Quantifying & Mitigating Data Leakage in Vector Embeddings via DP**<br>*Badi et al.* | Outlines exactly how membership inference extracts precise sentence structures from dense vectors, mapped specifically to language models. | Establishing the precise bound of $\epsilon$ is difficult without rendering the vectorized threat completely unsearchable. |
| 19 | **IEEE TMC**<br>2023 | **Local Differential Privacy for Deep Learning and Edge Computing**<br>*Chen et al.* | Applies LDP to neural network gradient updates explicitly during the FL optimization passes. | Adding noise locally at every node severely damages the final global classification accuracy on complex datasets. |
| 20 | **C&S**<br>2023 | **Generative Red Teaming: Integrating LLMs for Automated Pen Testing**<br>*Qiao et al.* | Generates adversarial inputs programmatically via an LLM agent attempting to bypass validation logic. | Essential for "testing" immunity outputs, but the Generator agent can suffer from collapse and loop repeated test vectors. |
| 21 | **IEEE S&P**<br>2023 | **Agentic AI Frameworks for Automated Cyber Defense Mechanisms**<br>*Yuan, Zheng, et al.* | Conceptually validates translation of abstract natural language mitigations into direct firewall (`iptables`/ModSecurity) rules. | High unmitigated risk if a hallucinated rule causes an internal self-inflicted Denial of Service (dropping valid packets). |
| 22 | **IEEE TAI**<br>2023 | **RAG for Dynamic Knowledge Graphs in Cyber Threat Intelligence**<br>*Chen, Liu, et al.* | Merges vector cosine similarity with semantic graph edge traversals, ensuring incident matches actually have linked root causes. | Vastly more complex implementation overhead to maintain dual-state databases (Graph + Vector) synchronicity. |
| 23 | **JNCA**<br>2023 | **Automating Defense Infrastructure (Immunity) via Threat Pattern Retrieval**<br>*Vasylenko et al.* | Semantic matching across distinct companies identifies identical persistent actors moving laterally. | Sharing raw heuristic traces still risks secondary inference of corporate assets unless strictly scrubbed. |
| 24 | **C&S**<br>2023 | **Applying Semantic Vector Databases for Threat Actor Profiling...**<br>*Benchea et al.* | Clusters isolated network events into contiguous incident "campaigns" based entirely on vector space proximity. | Advanced actors rotating IPs and changing TTPs frequently evade dense-vector clustering constraints. |
| 25 | **IEEE IoT**<br>2022 | **Federated-Learning-Based Anomaly Detection for IoT Security**<br>*Mothukuri et al.* | Standard baseline FL approach implemented specifically against modern IoT network captures. | Demonstrates limits of basic Deep Learning architectures against highly variable IoT botnet traffic. |
| 26 | **IEEE Network**<br>2022 | **Federated Learning for Industrial IoT: Framework, Applications...**<br>*Zhang, Chen, et al.* | Summarizes architectural requirements (synchrony, bandwidth) for integrating FL into critical SCADA frameworks. | Time-sensitive synchronization requirements often force asynchronous aggregation, adding complexity. |
| 27 | **C&S**<br>2022 | **Intelligent Mechanism for Enhancing IDS using Federated Deep Learning**<br>*Alassery et al.* | Benchmarks varying MLP and CNN layer depths for distributed feature processing in IDS configurations. | Deeper classification heads result in significantly slower local training times per FL round. |
| 28 | **IEEE TDSC**<br>2022 | **Trust-Aware Federated Learning Framework for Reliable Distributed Systems**<br>*Zhang et al.* | Mathematical definition of behavioral bounding specifically intercepting Sybil and data-poisoning variants before aggregation. | Reactive methodology; struggles against attacks that poison the validation set the trust metric is derived from. |
| 29 | **IEEE TRel**<br>2022 | **FedRobust: Robust Federated Learning with Byzantine Nodes**<br>*Yin et al.* | Isolates coordinated Byzantine updates using median aggregation arrays rather than simple means. | Median aggregations are significantly more computationally expensive for the central server when running high-dimensionality MLPs. |
| 30 | **ACM TIST**<br>2022 | **Defending against Malicious Clients in FL via Robust Aggregation**<br>*Wu et al.* | Explores bounds of model stability; verifies how many concurrent malicious nodes can exist before structural model collapse. | Shows that if $>33\%$ of participating nodes collude, robust metrics consistently fail to isolate the threat. |
| 31 | **IEEE TNNLS**<br>2022 | **Privacy and Robustness in Federated Learning: Attacks and Defenses**<br>*Lyu, Yu, et al.* | Complete mapping of the attack surface facing central aggregation servers regarding data inversion. | Points out the persistent tug-of-war between strong cryptographic privacy measures and high utility. |
| 32 | **IEEE TII**<br>2021 | **DeepFed: Federated Deep Learning for IDS in Industrial CPS**<br>*Li et al.* | Uses gated recurrent units (GRUs) within FL to understand the temporal sequence of malicious packets. | Sequential models like GRUs/LSTMs are heavily impacted by transmission delays in FL loops, breaking state continuity. |
| 33 | **IEEE TIFS**<br>2021 | **Mitigating Sybils in Federated Learning Poisoning**<br>*Fung, Yoon...* | Details Sybil attack lifecycle in FL, noting how malicious nodes build temporary "trust" before flipping labels. | Defense mechanisms fail if the Sybil nodes completely randomize their flip intervals, avoiding heuristic detection. |
| 34 | **IEEE/ACM ToN**<br>2021 | **Efficient and Privacy-Preserving FL with Differential Privacy**<br>*Hao, Li, et al.* | Identifies optimal thresholds for noise injection during network dataset processing specifically. | Too small $\sigma$ fails to mask outliers; too high $\sigma$ degrades the final model to lower accuracy than local sets. |
| 35 | **ACM AIS**<br>2021 | **Hybrid Approach to Privacy-Preserving Federated Learning**<br>*Truex et al.* | Integrates Secure Multi-Party Computation overlapping with FL for extreme privacy preservation. | MPC drastically increases networking load per round, causing severe scaling issues beyond a few nodes. |
| 36 | **MLSys**<br>2020 | **Federated Optimization in Heterogeneous Networks (FedProx)**<br>*Li, Sahu, et al.* | **Groundbreaking Algorithm:** Solves the primary non-IID data issue using proximal term ($\mu$) gradient constraint limiting localized drift. | The $\mu$ parameter is difficult to tune globally; an incorrect setup severely stalls the entire architecture's convergence. |
| 37 | **NeurIPS**<br>2020 | **Personalized Federated Learning with Theoretical Guarantees...**<br>*Fallah, Mokhtari...* | Evaluates Model-Agnostic Meta-Learning as a layer allowing each node to customize the final model slightly for their unique environment. | Nodes with low local data volume fail to properly fit their personalized layers, leaving them vulnerable to unique local attacks. |
| 38 | **IEEE TIFS**<br>2020 | **Federated Learning with Differential Privacy: Algorithms & Performance**<br>*Wei et al.* | Foundational proof of quantifiable $\epsilon$-DP limits guaranteeing mathematical non-reversibility of shared updates. | Outlines the strict "Privacy-Accuracy" spectrum showing the inescapable mathematical toll noise injection takes on precision. |
